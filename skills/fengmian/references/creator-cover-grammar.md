# Creator cover grammar

Use this reference when selecting a creator-cover concept; reuse its criteria for final inspection without rereading unchanged text.

## Genre gate

Default to a personal creator thumbnail:

- oversized short headline
- one oversized topic prop or product relationship
- one photographic creator performing a readable action
- one quiet high-contrast background world
- strong white, yellow, black, and topic-color separation

Avoid drifting into these neighboring genres unless the user explicitly requests them:

- cinematic product advertisement
- SaaS landing-page hero
- polished dashboard showcase
- editorial technology campaign
- realistic workstation scene whose environment becomes the main subject

The creator-cover genre may use polished 3D objects and realistic light, but the result must still read as a creator explaining one thing at phone-thumbnail size.

## Four-signal alignment

Make four signals repeat the same meaning.

| Signal | Job | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline | Names the topic and one value | Can the topic be named from the words alone? |
| Dominant prop | Makes the topic visible | Does one object explain the subject without labels? |
| Person action | Explains the relationship | Is the creator visibly using, holding, revealing, or presenting the prop? |
| Background world | Establishes place and emotion | Does it support the topic without adding a second explanation? |

Reject a route when any signal points to a different story. Do not let the background become a fifth explainer.

## Three-center budget

Allow three primary visual centers at most:

1. Headline
2. Combined person-prop action
3. Optional proof strip

Treat multiple product icons as one grouped prop when their relationship is the subject. Supporting details must disappear under slight blur without damaging comprehension.

## Background withdrawal

Use the background as a stage:

- one color field, simple room, sky, office, desk, or topic world
- one broad glow or depth cue behind the person-prop cluster
- low contrast under the headline
- no feature explanations in the background

Delete floating documents, network nodes, grids, light trails, UI cards, server panels, repeated shelves, and dashboard controls unless one of them is the single dominant prop.

When the background feels empty, strengthen scale, crop, lighting, or the main prop before adding decorations.

## Main prop and person action

Translate the abstract topic into one oversized physical object. Useful examples include a manual, binder, phone, toolbox, cockpit, bridge, shelf, folder, or finished result.

Give the creator one primary action and at least two visible interaction cues for tutorial covers:

- support plus point
- hold plus gaze
- operate plus visible feedback
- open plus reveal
- compare plus choose

Keep facial expression natural by default. Put energy into posture, arm direction, crop, prop scale, contrast, and scene consequence.

Reject tiny fingertip actions, distant icon taps, or decorative pointing. The action must survive at 10 percent size.

## Two-tool tutorial pattern

When two tools combine to produce one result, group them into one cause-and-effect prop:

`tool A + tool B -> visible result`

Use official icons as large mounted, held, or clearly connected parts of the dominant prop. Make the creator physically bridge the relationship through two hands, a support-plus-point pose, or one operation with visible feedback.

For a tutorial where two generic tools create one knowledge library:

- useful headline: `个人AI知识库` plus `从零跑通`
- useful dominant prop: one oversized binder, knowledge shelf, or finished library with two neutral tool symbols attached as a single system
- useful action: one hand supports or opens the knowledge object while the other connects the two tool symbols; gaze follows the result
- useful background: a clean topic-matched field with one activation glow
- reject: cinematic machinery, small floating icon cards, tiny connector taps, dense interface panels, or a product-demo scene that needs close inspection

## Rejection gates

Reject and regenerate before polishing when any answer is no:

1. At 10 percent size, can the viewer name the topic within one second?
2. Is the headline clearly the first focal point?
3. Is there one dominant prop rather than a collection of feature objects?
4. Does the person visibly change, support, operate, reveal, or present the prop?
5. Do at least two interaction cues remain readable when the cover contains a tutorial creator?
6. Does the background stay quieter than the headline and action cluster?
7. Does the cover look like creator content rather than a product advertisement?
8. If the title is hidden, do the prop and person action still suggest the correct subject category?
9. If the person is removed, does the explanation lose an essential action?
10. Under slight blur, do only the headline and person-prop cluster remain?

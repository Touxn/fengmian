# Creator cover visual grammar

Use these principles to express the selected promise. They do not impose a fixed poster template.

## Let the useful content lead

The main focal point can be a finished result, a comparison, a person, an object, or text. Choose the element that makes the promise most visible. A large title, large portrait, giant prop, and logo are all optional unless requested.

Create hierarchy through size, grouping, crop, contrast, and negative space. Keep secondary elements subordinate. Count perceptual groups rather than individual details; do not impose a universal element budget. Remove an element when it competes with the promise or adds no useful information.

## Give each element a job

| Element | Possible job |
| --- | --- |
| Finished output | Demonstrate appearance, capability, or a relevant outcome |
| Comparison | Make a meaningful difference visible |
| Person | Recognition, trust, explanation, emotion, or action |
| Short phrase | Frame a benefit, question, or decision |
| Product name or logo | Identify a relevant tool or relationship |
| Arrow or connector | Clarify an otherwise ambiguous direction or transformation |
| Background | Context, separation, or atmosphere |

Do not make every element repeat the same information. A supporting element may build recognition without needing a physical action.

## Make evidence readable

Enlarge the relevant part of a real result or interface when necessary. Preserve the relationship that makes a comparison honest and understandable. Two states can form one main visual group.

Cinematic imagery, workbench scenes, UI crops, editorial cutouts, and physical metaphors are valid choices. Evaluate their ability to communicate at small size. Do not reject an entire visual category or prefer decorative world-building over stronger evidence.

A spectacular generated picture may illustrate a concept. Do not imply it is an actual result from the featured tool unless that provenance is established.

## Contrast and typography

Use enough separation for the main subject and wording to remain legible at the intended display size. Choose palette and outline treatments according to the image. Blue/purple backgrounds, yellow/white text, glow, and heavy outlines are available treatments, not universal requirements.

Keep complete Chinese glyphs, semantic line breaks, and adequate spacing. Avoid shrinking text to preserve a decorative layout. Approved text remains exact; when wording is delegated, remove redundancy before enlarging the text block.

The image can carry most of the message. A cover may use little or no text when the publishing title provides the needed context. Typography does not automatically outrank visible results.

## People and relationships

Use [person integration](person-integration.md) when a person is present. A portrait, relevant reaction, or useful gaze can communicate the relationship without contact. Choose an appropriate expression and preserve approved identity and expression during revisions.

When the selected design includes physical action, make contact, anatomy, support, perspective, and lighting plausible. Do not invent an action merely to justify including the host.

For two tools, show the relevant relationship or combined result clearly. A simple comparison, connector, or shared output can work. Do not force the host to hold both tools or link them with both hands.

## Review questions

1. What is the first focal point, and does it support the video's main promise?
2. What evidence or meaningful situation can the viewer recognize at the intended size?
3. Do the image, cover wording, and publishing title contribute useful information?
4. Is any important element competing with unrelated detail?
5. Is the curiosity relevant while the basic topic remains understandable?
6. Does the composition preserve the requested identity, wording, and accepted work?
7. Does the actual content support the implied result and emotional intensity?

Use [layout specification](layout-spec.md) for display-size and format checks. Visual inspection can establish readability and reveal defects; it cannot establish a click-through improvement. Use [evidence and testing](evidence-and-testing.md) when performance feedback is requested.

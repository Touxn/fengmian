# Person integration

Read this reference when a cover contains a person. Choose how the person contributes to the content promise before choosing a pose. A clear portrait, relevant reaction, or useful gaze can work without physical contact.

## Choose a role before a pose

Possible roles include:

| Role | Useful contribution |
| --- | --- |
| Guide or analyst | Trust, recognition, interpretation |
| Tester or comparator | A decision, experiment, or comparison |
| Operator or builder | An action connected to a visible outcome |
| Witness or reaction | Emotion that makes the result meaningful |
| Character inside an output | Evidence of generated quality or continuity |

These are optional examples, not required proposal slots. Select the contribution that fits the video. A recognizable host may provide continuity without a gesture. If the person is optional and hides stronger evidence, reduce or omit the portrait. Preserve an explicit user requirement to include the person.

## Visual relationship

- Let gaze, expression, scale, and position support the main promise.
- The person may be secondary to a result, or primary when a reaction or identity carries the story.
- Use one clear relationship between the person and the result, object, or comparison. Avoid competing gestures and unrelated gaze targets.
- In a continuous scene, make light, perspective, scale, and depth coherent.
- In an intentional split screen, framed portrait, or editorial cutout, the parts may remain visually distinct. Make the separation deliberate and legible.
- Use outlines, shadows, or edge contrast when they improve separation. A cutout treatment is valid when it suits the composition.
- Do not require a contact pose, an interaction count, a closed triangle, or a particular side of the canvas.

## Physical interaction when depicted

Apply these checks only where the design depicts contact or physical action:

- Connect the hand, wrist, forearm, and shoulder into one plausible pose.
- Check visible fingers and occlusion. Do not force all five fingers into view.
- Make grips, support, contact shadows, and object perspective consistent with the action.
- Judge weight from material, balance, posture, and support. Object width alone does not determine weight.
- Remove accidental air gaps, doubled edges, or glow seams at contact points.
- Keep the gesture and its target understandable at thumbnail size. Simplify or enlarge a gesture that disappears.
- A pointing gesture should identify a meaningful target, without an arbitrary fingertip-distance rule.
- If an interaction needs repair, respect protected regions and the approved baseline. Do not attach disconnected limbs or silently regenerate unrelated areas.

## Face, expression, and light

- Use the original portrait as identity authority. A generated accepted face may guide placement and expression, but does not replace the original identity reference.
- Preserve facial proportions, age, glasses, hair, complexion, and recognizable features.
- Choose an expression appropriate to the content and the user's request. Preserve an approved expression during local revisions.
- Match facial energy to the content. Do not manufacture an extreme reaction just to imitate a thumbnail convention.
- Reject generic beautification, face slimming, enlarged eyes, distorted glasses, patchy skin, and face/neck discontinuity.
- Inspect identity at a useful full-size or enlarged view. Thumbnail legibility cannot establish likeness.
- For a continuous scene, reconcile light direction, color cast, shadow softness, and nearby reflected light. For a graphic layout, judge each panel's coherence and the overall visual treatment without forcing a shared fictional light source.

## Wardrobe and continuity

- Preserve the accepted outfit during local edits and across ratios in the same campaign unless the user requests a change.
- For a new topic, choose clothing that suits the role, subject, and known publication context. Use a climate-neutral choice when context is absent; routine covers do not require location or weather research.
- Reference clothing is optional for a new composition unless the user approved it as part of the target campaign.
- A recurring outfit can strengthen recognition. Variation can keep a series fresh. Choose deliberately without requiring a fixed number of changed garment properties.
- Keep garments free of invented affiliations, logos, and decorative unreadable text.

## Prompt construction

State the person's role and contribution first, for example:

`The host provides recognition and looks toward the finished result, which remains the main focal point.`

Then specify only the details needed for this composition:

- Identity authority and the limited roles of pose or layout references
- Gaze, expression, scale, and placement
- Required outfit and campaign continuity
- Continuous scene, split screen, portrait frame, or cutout treatment
- Contact, weight, and anatomy where physical interaction is depicted
- Permitted changes and protected regions

Do not add physical action solely to satisfy a template.

## Revisions and validation

Use [revision-control.md](revision-control.md) for accepted baselines, operation selection, and protected-region checks. Use [layout-spec.md](layout-spec.md) for format and thumbnail checks.

- For a non-face edit, compare the candidate with the accepted face at the same scale. Drift in proportions, glasses, expression, or complexion fails the revision.
- For a face edit, use the user-selected scene as target and original portrait as identity authority. Preserve approved head position, angle, and expression unless asked to change them.
- If repeated repairs lose likeness, return to the accepted baseline and change the preservation strategy.
- Confirm that the person contributes recognition, trust, emotion, context, or evidence without obscuring the main promise.
- Check identity, outfit continuity, and composition treatment separately from small-size readability.
- Check anatomy, weight, and contact only when the depicted action requires them.
- Do not infer click performance from the presence of a face, an expressive pose, or a legible thumbnail.
